2. The "Broadness" Fallacy vs. Epistemic Normative Precision

General-purpose LLMs are trained to generate conversational text across infinite domains. When asked to police digital community discourse, they fail across two structural dimensions:

Failure Mode A
The OpenAI Moderation API Trap
  • Purely Binary Legal Liability Filter: Designed exclusively to catch extreme legal liabilities: hate speech, violence, and self-harm.
  • Zero Constructiveness Modeling: Cannot distinguish between an evidence-based thesis and low-effort platitudes.
  • Blind to Cynicism & Whataboutism: An insult like “You're an absolute amateur who understands nothing about economics” is marked flagged: false because it does not attack a protected demographic.
  • No In-Composer Coaching: Merely returns true/false post-submission. Zero inline suggestions or constructive diffs.
Normative Solution
Continuous 4D Epistemic Rubric
  • Constructiveness (35%): Quantifies empirical data, citations, and solution-oriented alternatives.
  • Civility (25%): Distinguishes ferocious intellectual dissent from ad-hominem vitriol.
  • Persuasiveness (20%): Assesses internal logical consistency and epistemic grounding.
  • Relevance (20%): Suppresses deflection, spam, and bad-faith whataboutism.
  • Inline Rephrase Coaching: Generates real-time, git-style diffs before readers hit submit.
⚠️ Non-Determinism & Sycophancy Bias in Cloud LLMs
Prompting Gemini or GPT-4o for discourse evaluation introduces epistemic non-determinism. The identical comment evaluated twice can yield different scores. Furthermore, general LLMs exhibit severe sycophancy bias: rewarding polite, verbose comments that lack factual substance, while penalizing rigorous scientific refutations that employ forceful academic critique.
